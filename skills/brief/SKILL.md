---

Dor needs a **brief**: a simple presentation of the real work, told the way the person who did it would tell a colleague. Dor's latest words set the scope.

1. **Find the context.** Read what Dor means from Dor's words and the moment you are in: the fixes you just made, a test, a decision, a sprint, a whole session, or anything else. There is no fixed size. Brief that context, as large or as small as it is. When this conversation has no work yet, use the session file or sprint Dor points at; with none, ask Dor which work Dor means.
2. **Check its real state.** Check what the work touches: its `SPRINT.md`, its epic, its files, git, live systems. A fact you cannot check is unknown. Change nothing.
3. **Present it.** Open with the point: where this leaves Dor and what it means. Each finding carries its consequence: what changes for Dor because of it. Cut whatever changes neither. Cut what that context doesn't need; a point Dor already heard comes back only when the context needs it. Answer what Dor asked, in the shape that answer needs. Write it as your own telling, so the content sets the structure.

   Draw on these only where they hold something Dor needs to know:
   - What Dor wanted, in Dor's words.
   - What you did and what came back: the real results, numbers and names.
   - What went well, with its result.
   - What failed and matters: what broke, why, and whether it's fixed.
   - What is blocked: why, and who can unblock it.
   - What is next: the exact step, and who takes it.

   Every claim shows the work itself: "The live site refuses the leaked key, but 33 old Vercel URLs still accept it and return the full Drive list." Say only what happened. The length follows the work.

Done when Dor can tell what happened in that context and what it came to, with the real results.
